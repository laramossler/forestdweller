Forest restoration does not happen quickly.

The invasive ivy we're removing has been growing for 30 years. The native trees we're planting will take 50 years to mature. The soil we're trying to rebuild loses organic matter faster than it accumulates. Success, if it comes at all, will not happen in my lifetime.

## The Speed of Modern Life

Everything in modern life is designed for speed and immediate results. Software deploys in minutes. Meals arrive in seconds. Metrics update in real-time. We've built a civilization optimized for velocity.

But forests don't work that way. Trees measure time in decades. Soil measures time in centuries. Ecosystems measure time in millennia.

## Learning to Wait

Working with the land requires accepting a different pace:

- Plant a tree, wait 50 years for shade
- Remove invasive species, wait 3 years to see what returns naturally
- Build a shelter with hand tools, accept that it takes months instead of days
- Restore degraded soil, understand that meaningful change takes decades

This is antithetical to modern productivity culture. There are no growth metrics. No quarterly goals. No rapid iteration cycles.

Progress is measured in seasons, not sprints.

## Why This Matters

I think we've forgotten how to work slowly. We've lost the ability to start projects that won't be finished in our lifetimes. We've abandoned work that can't be measured, optimized, and scaled.

Forest restoration is teaching me that some work is worth doing even if I never see the results. Some projects are worth starting even if they take 100 years to complete. Some progress is worth making even if it can't be captured in a metric.

## Resistance

This website exists as a small act of resistance to that pressure for speed and scale.

No algorithms optimizing for engagement. No metrics dashboard tracking growth. No A/B tests to maximize clicks. Just slow documentation of slow work, published when there's something worth sharing.

If this project is still online in 50 years, documenting the gradual return of a healthy forest ecosystem, that will be success enough.
